Grease rules in Winston-Salem, in plain words.

Like most North Carolina cities, Winston-Salem expects food businesses to keep grease out of the sewer system — that is what FOG (fats, oils, and grease) programs are for. The standard that drives your cleaning schedule is the 25% rule: when grease and solids pass a quarter of your trap's depth, the trap stops doing its job and grease starts reaching the city line.

Inspectors ask for records. Every cleaning we do comes with a signed manifest showing when your trap was serviced and where the grease went. Keep them in a folder by the office door and inspection day gets a lot shorter.

North Carolina also requires any company that pumps and hauls grease to hold a state Septage Management Firm permit. Ask any company that quotes your trap — including us — for its permit number before you book.

Winston-Salem grease trap questions.

How often should a grease trap be cleaned?

The standard most NC grease programs use is the 25% rule: when grease and solids build up past a quarter of the trap's depth, it is time — past that point, grease starts passing through to the city line. Busy fryers fill faster. We will help you set a schedule that keeps you ahead of it.

How much does grease trap cleaning cost in Winston-Salem?

It depends on four things: the size of your trap, how easy it is to reach, how full it is, and how often it is cleaned. Traps on a regular schedule cost less to maintain than traps that only get attention when they back up. Call us and we will quote your trap straight — no surprises.

What does a cleaning include?

A full pump-out — not a skim. We remove the grease, water, and solids, scrape down the walls and baffles, check the flow, and leave you a signed manifest for your records.

What if my trap backs up or starts to smell?

Call us. A sour smell or a slow drain is the trap telling you it is full. Regular cleaning stops most backups before they start — and a backup during service costs far more than a pump-out.

Do you give paperwork for inspections?

Yes. Every visit comes with a signed manifest. Keep it with your records — it is the first thing a grease inspector asks for.
